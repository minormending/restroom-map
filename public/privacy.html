Privacy

Last updated 14 September 2026

Not reviewed by a lawyer. This page was drafted to describe this system accurately, which is not the same as being legally sufficient where you operate.

Restroom Map is a map of publicly usable bathrooms that people contribute to. This page describes exactly what it collects and what it deliberately does not.

What is collected

If you sign in

Signing in uses Google. Google gives us your account identifier, email address, display name and profile picture link. We do not see or receive your Google password.

What you contribute

Places, door codes, reports that something works or doesn't, and notes. These are public, and shown next to your display name.

Location

If you allow it, your browser's position is used to centre the map. When you report that a bathroom works, that position is also sent so the server can check whether you were near it.

Those coordinates are not stored. The server computes a single yes/no — were you within about 150 metres — records that, and discards the position. There is no column anywhere holding where you were, and no location history exists to request or to leak.

Network address

To stop one person flooding the map with false reports, a salted one-way hash of your IP address is kept as a counter. It is not stored alongside anything you submitted, it cannot be turned back into an address on its own, and the counters are deleted after two days.

Kept in your browser, not sent anywhere

  • Your sign-in session, so you stay signed in.
  • Which places you reported recently, so the buttons stop asking.

What is never done

  • No advertising, ad networks, tracking pixels or analytics of any kind.
  • No location history, and no profile of where you go.
  • Nothing sold, rented or shared for marketing.

This matters more here than on most sites. Where somebody looks for a bathroom can imply things about their health, and the safest way to protect that is to not have the data.

Who else is involved

  • Supabase — database and sign-in. Data is held in the United States (US East).
  • GitHub Pages — serves the site itself.
  • CARTO — map imagery. Your browser requests map tiles from them directly.
  • Komoot (Photon) — place search. What you type into the search box is sent to them.
  • Google — sign-in only.

Public versus private

Public: places, codes, reports, notes, and your display name.

Not public: your email address, your credit balance and history, and which codes you have unlocked.

Deleting your data

Open an issue on the project’s issue tracker and your account, notes and reports will be deleted. The tracker is public, so say what you want done and nothing more — do not post your email address there. You will be asked on the issue for whatever is needed to find the account.

Places you added may be kept without your name attached, because other people’s reports depend on them — say so if you would rather they were removed entirely.

You can also revoke this app's access from your Google account settings at any time.

Children

This site is not directed at children under 13 and accounts are not knowingly created for them.
